## Open sources and make your decisions

1. [SEC company search](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/) → company → **10-K** →
   **Business** and **Earnings per share**; or its investor-relations annual results.
2. [Nasdaq market activity](https://www.nasdaq.com/market-activity/stocks) → ticker → quote
   or historical data. Match the target and peers' trading dates.
3. Record price, annual reported diluted earnings per share, fiscal year-end, publication
   date and source locator. Earnings must have been public by your valuation date.
4. Decide **use / qualify / exclude**, with a business reason.

**Expect:** sources you opened and decisions you can explain. An AI list is only a lead.

## Validate — check one peer decision

1. Check one admitted peer's price divided by earnings per share by hand.
2. Predict the effect of removing one peer, then read that result from your calculator.
3. Explain the price change and loss of information. Do not reject a peer to improve the answer.

One remaining peer gives a reference; removing your sole peer leaves no estimate.
If your company's P/E is unusable, explain the sourced obstacle and what evidence would
resolve it. No substitute case is required.

## Reflect — defend your conclusion

Explain to your partner:

1. Why your peers belong, including their differences.
2. What their comparison adds to your DCF—or why P/E cannot support one.
3. Your **initiate / watch-defer / do not initiate** decision and defensible range or limitation.
4. What evidence would change your decision.

Answer your partner's skeptical question. Keep the conclusion in your `.md`.
Do not mechanically average the methods.
