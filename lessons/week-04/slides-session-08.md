## The worked trio

**EV/EBITDA** prices the whole operating business; **P/E** prices the shareholders' claim.
Same target, same day, three defensible routes:

| Route | Multiple | Per share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Trading EV/EBITDA | 13× | **$15.80** |
| Trading P/E | 15× | **$12.00** |
| Precedent deal | 14× | **$17.40** |

**Why does the deal sit above trading?** Because a buyer of the *whole company* pays for
things a minority share never gets: **control**, **expected synergies**, a **competitive
auction**, and a **particular moment in time**.

**Why is averaging malpractice?** Split the difference between the enterprise route and the
deal and you get **$16.60**. Average all three and you get **$15.07**. Both are arithmetically
fine and analytically empty — each one deletes the exact thing you were hired to explain, and
the second one buries a control premium inside a decimal. **The spread is the finding.**

*This training case is its own company, not Week 3's DCF target. Practice the moves here;
triangulate only your own company's numbers.*

## Deal evidence has a timestamp

A **precedent transaction** is an announced or completed acquisition of a whole company, used
as evidence of what a buyer paid for control of a similar business. **LTM** = last twelve
months — the trailing period a denominator is measured over.

**Use only what was knowable at announcement.** Closing evidence answers *status*; it does not
make announcement-date inputs retroactively knowable. **Defend the deal's context, or set it
aside.**

> The shape, not your answer *(from the packet's deal)*: "**Knowable at announcement:** the
> election terms — **$142.50 in cash or 0.25200 Broadcom shares per VMware share**, subject to
> **50/50 aggregate proration** — because they sit in the **Form 8-K dated May 26, 2022**.
> Valuing the stock leg needs a *price date*, and the release used Broadcom's **May 25, 2022**
> close to state **$138.23** per VMware share.
> **Not knowable at announcement:** that the deal would close, or when. The **November 22,
> 2023** completion tells me the deal's **status** and nothing a May-2022 analyst could have
> used. Treating it as if it did is hindsight wearing a source."

> **And the denominator you must refuse.** The announcement's approximately **$8.5 billion**
> is *projected combined contribution within three years after closing* — **not VMware's LTM
> EBITDA at announcement**. Dividing the **$69 billion** headline bridge (**≈$61bn** equity
> transaction value **+ ≈$8bn** assumed net debt) by it produces a multiple of nothing.

## The synthetic precedent, by hand

**Predict first, then compute.** The qualified precedent closed at **14×** against a trading
median of **13×** — one extra turn. Before you touch a calculator: how much per share is one
turn worth here? Commit to a number.

> EV = 14.0 × 80 = **1,120** → bridge: 1,120 + 50 − 300 = **870** → 870 / 50 = **$17.40**

**One turn of EBITDA = 80 of enterprise value = $1.60 per share** on 50M shares — which is
exactly the gap between $15.80 and $17.40. A precedent multiple is not a different kind of
number. It is the same arithmetic, applied to evidence with **control and a date baked into
it** — and that is the part you have to defend.

## The 45-second recommendation

To a partner acting as your investment committee. Formal, standing, no notes if you can:

> **Action** · **range** · **which evidence best fits *this* question** · **condition** ·
> **reversal trigger.** Then take **one skeptical question.**

> The shape, not your answer *(the training case, in 45 seconds)*: "**Watch-defer** for a
> committee with no current position. **My range is $12.00 to $17.40 a share, and I am not
> averaging it.** The two trading routes bracket **$12.00 to $15.80**; the precedent adds
> **$17.40** above them. **The evidence that fits your question is the trading comparables** —
> you asked what a minority stake is worth today, and only the trading routes are measured on
> today's date and today's claim. The **$17.40** is what one buyer paid for control and
> synergies in one auction: a **ceiling**, not a price. Inside the trading pair I lean to the
> **$15.80** enterprise route, because **P/E** carries this target's leverage — **$300M** of
> debt against **$50M** of cash — and you are asking about the business, not the balance sheet.
> **Condition: I move to initiate below $12.00**, the lowest route any of my evidence supports.
> **Reversal trigger: the peer median moving a full turn.** One turn is **$1.60** a share here,
> so a median sliding **13× → 12×** takes my enterprise route to **$14.20** and closes the gap
> to my P/E route by nearly half — at which point the two trading lenses are saying the same
> thing and my recommendation changes."

*The skeptical question you will get: **"Why not just average them?"** Have that answer ready
in one sentence.*
