Use your Week 3 sources: how does your company earn money? Which businesses share those economics? Are its annual reported earnings positive? Name what you need to research.

Question: what would your company's share be worth at defensible peer multiples, and how does that compare with your DCF?

Represent — policy before names

Before AI: write your peer policy. Send it with your company and Week 3 valuation date. Investigate two candidates; while AI searches, write rejection criteria.

My target, comparison date and peer policy are below. Suggest at most two listed operating-company candidates. For each, give a primary-source link and section supporting its business model, one important difference from my target, and its latest annual reported diluted EPS public by my comparison date, with the fiscal period and publication date. Do not rank candidates or change my policy. Mark anything you cannot verify unresolved. I will open the sources and decide whether to use it.

Open sources and make your decisions

  1. SEC company search → company → 10-K → Business and Earnings per share; or its investor-relations annual results.
  2. Nasdaq market activity → ticker → quote or historical data. Match the target and peers' trading dates.
  3. Record price, annual reported diluted earnings per share, fiscal year-end, publication date and source locator. Earnings must have been public by your valuation date.
  4. Decide use / qualify / exclude, with a business reason.

Expect: sources you opened and decisions you can explain. An AI list is only a lead.

Implement — your company, your inputs

Replace the Asbury inputs with your target and only use / qualify peers. Keep excluded candidates and their reasons in your .md source table.

Terminal → run your saved command. Record the range or single-peer reference.

If annual earnings are zero or negative, cite the source and explain why P/E cannot support a valuation. Still investigate your peers. No alternative method is required for this lab. Missing data remains unresolved; identify what is missing.

Expect: your own sourced comparison or a specific, evidenced limitation.

Validate — check one peer decision

  1. Check one admitted peer's price divided by earnings per share by hand.
  2. Predict the effect of removing one peer, then read that result from your calculator.
  3. Explain the price change and loss of information. Do not reject a peer to improve the answer.

One remaining peer gives a reference; removing your sole peer leaves no estimate. If your company's P/E is unusable, explain the sourced obstacle and what evidence would resolve it. No substitute case is required.

Evolve — compare; ask AI; judge

Put your DCF and peer result beside each other using Lab 08's table. Send the table and provisional call to your resumed chat:

Review my valuation comparison as a skeptical colleague. Identify the weakest supported assumption and any mismatch in company, date, valuation object or earnings definition. Do not invent a missing range or average the methods. Ask one question that could change my decision. I will check your criticism against my sources before revising my call.

While AI responds, write what could change your mind. Check its criticism against your sources; record accept / reject / unresolved, with a reason.

Reflect — defend your conclusion

Explain to your partner:

  1. Why your peers belong, including their differences.
  2. What their comparison adds to your DCF—or why P/E cannot support one.
  3. Your initiate / watch-defer / do not initiate decision and defensible range or limitation.
  4. What evidence would change your decision.

Answer your partner's skeptical question. Keep the conclusion in your .md. Do not mechanically average the methods.
