The worked trio

EV/EBITDA prices the whole operating business; P/E prices the shareholders' claim. Same target, same day, three defensible routes:

The worked trio
Route Multiple Per share
Trading EV/EBITDA 13× $15.80
Trading P/E 15× $12.00
Precedent deal 14× $17.40

Why does the deal sit above trading? Because a buyer of the whole company pays for things a minority share never gets: control, expected synergies, a competitive auction, and a particular moment in time.

Why is averaging malpractice? Split the difference between the enterprise route and the deal and you get $16.60. Average all three and you get $15.07. Both are arithmetically fine and analytically empty — each one deletes the exact thing you were hired to explain, and the second one buries a control premium inside a decimal. The spread is the finding.

This training case is its own company, not Week 3's DCF target. Practice the moves here; triangulate only your own company's numbers.

Deal evidence has a timestamp

A precedent transaction is an announced or completed acquisition of a whole company, used as evidence of what a buyer paid for control of a similar business. LTM = last twelve months — the trailing period a denominator is measured over.

Use only what was knowable at announcement. Closing evidence answers status; it does not make announcement-date inputs retroactively knowable. Defend the deal's context, or set it aside.

The shape, not your answer (from the packet's deal): "Knowable at announcement: the election terms — $142.50 in cash or 0.25200 Broadcom shares per VMware share, subject to 50/50 aggregate proration — because they sit in the Form 8-K dated May 26, 2022. Valuing the stock leg needs a price date, and the release used Broadcom's May 25, 2022 close to state $138.23 per VMware share. Not knowable at announcement: that the deal would close, or when. The November 22, 2023 completion tells me the deal's status and nothing a May-2022 analyst could have used. Treating it as if it did is hindsight wearing a source."

And the denominator you must refuse. The announcement's approximately $8.5 billion is projected combined contribution within three years after closing — not VMware's LTM EBITDA at announcement. Dividing the $69 billion headline bridge (≈$61bn equity transaction value + ≈$8bn assumed net debt) by it produces a multiple of nothing.

The synthetic precedent, by hand

Predict first, then compute. The qualified precedent closed at 14× against a trading median of 13× — one extra turn. Before you touch a calculator: how much per share is one turn worth here? Commit to a number.

EV = 14.0 × 80 = 1,120 → bridge: 1,120 + 50 − 300 = 870 → 870 / 50 = $17.40

One turn of EBITDA = 80 of enterprise value = $1.60 per share on 50M shares — which is exactly the gap between $15.80 and $17.40. A precedent multiple is not a different kind of number. It is the same arithmetic, applied to evidence with control and a date baked into it — and that is the part you have to defend.

Audit the deal — full attention, one track

Three sessions running, you fired two AI tracks at once. Today you choose not to. The packet and the filings are the sources, and auditing evidence is full-attention work. Knowing when not to start a second track is the same skill as running two.

(AI stays open for anything you honestly disclose. Nothing in this block asks for it.)

  1. Pick your deal: the Broadcom/VMware source packet — Week 4 page on GitHub → real-deal-source-packet.md, whose three source links open straight to the filings — or a real deal of your own you can source to primary documents.
  2. Record it in this order: terms · announcement and close dates · status · value bridge · denominator definition · control and synergy context · comparability to your target.
  3. Label every value as equity, debt, enterprise-like, or denominator. Headline numbers are not interchangeable, and most reporting treats them as if they were.
  4. Decide: use · qualify · exclude — and say which, out loud, rather than quietly leaving a deal out.

The shape, not your answer (one qualified deal reading): "Broadcom/VMware — QUALIFY as context, EXCLUDE from my range. Terms and dates are clean and sourced: announced May 26, 2022, closed November 22, 2023; elections of $142.50 cash or 0.25200 Broadcom shares at 50/50 proration. The bridge is roughly $61 billion equity transaction value plus about $8 billion assumed net debt — a $69 billion enterprise-like headline, and the release itself calls those figures rounded estimates. What I do not have is a date-aligned denominator. Without VMware's announcement-date LTM EBITDA on a stated reported-or-adjusted convention there is no defensible multiple, and the $8.5 billion projected contribution is a different object, so I refuse it. Consequence: the deal informs my control discussion; it does not enter my range — and I say so."

The 45-second recommendation

To a partner acting as your investment committee. Formal, standing, no notes if you can:

Action · range · which evidence best fits this question · condition · reversal trigger. Then take one skeptical question.

The shape, not your answer (the training case, in 45 seconds): "Watch-defer for a committee with no current position. My range is $12.00 to $17.40 a share, and I am not averaging it. The two trading routes bracket $12.00 to $15.80; the precedent adds $17.40 above them. The evidence that fits your question is the trading comparables — you asked what a minority stake is worth today, and only the trading routes are measured on today's date and today's claim. The $17.40 is what one buyer paid for control and synergies in one auction: a ceiling, not a price. Inside the trading pair I lean to the $15.80 enterprise route, because P/E carries this target's leverage — $300M of debt against $50M of cash — and you are asking about the business, not the balance sheet. Condition: I move to initiate below $12.00, the lowest route any of my evidence supports. Reversal trigger: the peer median moving a full turn. One turn is $1.60 a share here, so a median sliding 13× → 12× takes my enterprise route to $14.20 and closes the gap to my P/E route by nearly half — at which point the two trading lenses are saying the same thing and my recommendation changes."

The skeptical question you will get: "Why not just average them?" Have that answer ready in one sentence.
