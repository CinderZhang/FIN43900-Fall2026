## Define/Discover — understand your company first

> What would my company's share be worth at defensible peer P/E multiples, and how does that
> compare with my discounted cash flow valuation?

Use your Week 3 sources. Explain how your company earns money, which businesses might share
those economics, and whether its reported annual earnings are positive. Name what you still
need to research. *Expect:* a focused search question about your company, not just a ticker list.

## Represent — write your policy, then find candidates

Without AI, write what business economics must match and which differences you would qualify
or exclude. Keep this initial policy visible. If you revise it, explain why before recalculating.

Send your company, Week 3 valuation date and policy to both AI partners independently.
Choose **two candidates to investigate**, not every name returned. While they search, write
what evidence would make you reject a candidate.

## Implement — apply the calculator to your company

1. In your `.md`, record target and peer prices on the **same trading date**, annual reported
   diluted earnings per share, fiscal year-end, publication date and source locator. Use earnings
   public by your Week 3 valuation date and compatible currency/share bases. Keep reported
   and adjusted earnings separate; one quarter is not a full year.
2. Enter your target and **only peers marked use or qualify** into the Lab 07 calculator.
   Retain excluded candidates and reasons in your source table.
3. **Terminal → run your saved command.** Record your company's implied range or single-peer
   reference. *Expect:* a result traced to your company's inputs, with no Asbury numbers mixed in.

**If annual earnings are zero or negative:** cite the source and explain why P/E cannot support
this valuation. Still investigate your two candidates and explain their business fit. Do not
force a positive result or switch methods for this lab. Missing data stays unresolved, with the
missing evidence identified. A blank without investigation is not a supported limitation.

## Validate — check sources and one peer decision

Check one admitted peer's price divided by earnings per share by hand. Predict what removing
one peer will do, then read that result from your calculator. Explain the change; do not exclude
an inconvenient peer just to improve the answer. One remaining peer gives a reference; removing
the sole usable peer leaves no estimate.

If your company's P/E is unusable, explain which sourced input or comparability problem prevents
the calculation and what evidence would resolve it. No substitute case or alternative model is
required. *Expect:* a checked result or a checked, specific limitation for your own company.

## Reflect — defend your own company's conclusion

Explain your peer choices, what the comparison adds to your DCF, and why the results differ
or cannot be compared. State **initiate / watch-defer / do not initiate**, the range you can
defend or why you withhold one, and what evidence would change your decision. Answer your
partner's skeptical question. Do not mechanically average the methods.

## Merit anchors — five criteria, 5 points each

**4** = one minor weakness; **2** = an important link incomplete; **1** = minimal;
**0** = missing or fabricated. Score one underlying defect once.

| Criterion | 5 | 3 | 0–1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem/decision | company, valuation date and peer policy explicit before selection | one boundary vague | generic comparison |
| Data/evidence | two candidate decisions supported by opened sources; price and earnings bases traced | core sources present but material comparability or period limitation poorly explained | unverified AI list |
| Validation | arithmetic and changed-peer result checked and interpreted, or unusable P/E diagnosed from sources | checks present but interpretation shallow | unchecked output or unsupported blank |
| Financial judgment | explains DCF comparison, defensible range or justified withholding, and conditional action | plausible but weakly connected call | mechanical average or unsupported certainty |
| Explanation/transfer | own causal explanation and source-checked judgment of AI criticism | understandable but mainly descriptive | copied output without ownership |

A sourced explanation that P/E cannot value your company is judged by the same evidence and
reasoning standard as a usable result. No alternative valuation method is required in this lab.
