## What is in them

**Part 1** builds the base case from three annual reports: what the history does and does not tell you,
nineteen assumptions with a label on each one saying where it came from, one year built line by line
with cash computed last, checks that fail on purpose, five years of statements that balance, and one
value per share.

**Part 2** takes it apart: Asbury's beta measured from sixty months of prices, the same statements at
seven discount rates, what the terminal value assumes, every assumption shocked, what the market price
would require to be right, the pictures, and how to tell colleagues, answer their questions and defend
the work.

## From the instructor

I could have recorded these two videos myself in about two hours. Instead I made them with AI, and it
took me three days.

That is not a complaint. The three days did not produce two videos. They produced a valuation model with
forty-three tests, a notebook that is the screen you see in the video, a transcript that an independent
AI checked number by number against that screen, two independent reviews of the terminal value that
disagreed with me, slides built from the notebook, and a pipeline that turns the next company into videos
in an afternoon. The two hours would have produced two videos. Learning to work with AI was the point for
me, and the lesson is the one you will meet too: the first time is slow, because you are building the
process, not the product. The second time the process is the asset.

## What to do with this

Do it for your own company. Build the model with your AI partner. Label every assumption: history,
guidance, or judgment. Make the model print its checks — assets equal liabilities plus equity in every
projected year, cash on the balance sheet equal to the cash-flow statement, cash at or above your floor —
and make the valuation refuse to run when they fail. Then take it apart the way Part 2 does. When I open
your work, the check block is the first thing I look for.

The narration is my voice, synthesized with my consent, from my own notes; the disclaimer at the start
of each video says so.
