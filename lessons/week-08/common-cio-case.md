# Week 8 Common CIO Case — Meridian Foundation Reserve

This fictional, course-authored case is the only case used for the Lab 13 two-system comparison.
It is course practice, not evidence about your own Project 2. Do not add facts that are not stated.

## Decision request and dated facts

As of **October 15, 2026**, the CIO of the fictional Meridian Foundation asks an analyst to propose
a policy allocation and validation plan for a **$100 million reserve**. The committee will decide
whether to authorize analysis and a small pilot—not whether to execute trades today.

- The policy horizon is seven years, with quarterly review and annual rebalancing.
- The stated objective is **CPI + 4% net of estimated costs over rolling five-year periods**. It is
  an objective, not a guaranteed outcome.
- The reserve distributes **$750,000 at the end of each calendar quarter**. At least twelve months
  of scheduled distributions must remain in cash or daily-liquid government securities.
- The policy benchmark is **60% broad global equities / 40% U.S. investment-grade bonds**.
- The portfolio must be long-only, with no borrowing, short sales, or derivatives.
- A single issuer may not exceed 8%; annual turnover should not exceed 25% unless the committee
  approves a documented exception.
- At least 90% of the portfolio must be redeemable within five business days under ordinary
  market conditions.
- The CIO says, “The reserve cannot have a calendar-year loss greater than 10%.” No enforcement
  rule, probability, or response to an unavoidable breach is supplied.
- The CIO provides no sector, climate, social, tax, active-management, or home-bias preference.
- The committee has not defined whether the pilot should optimize expected return, tracking
  error, drawdown risk, probability of meeting distributions, or another measurable tradeoff.

## Human-first task

Before AI, classify each statement as an objective, hard constraint, preference, aspiration, or
unverifiable promise. Draft the decision user, action, universe, horizon, benchmark, risk measure,
liquidity rule, at least four operational constraints, naive baseline, validation plan, and the
minimum question that must be escalated before optimization.

## Common two-system prompt

Give each accessible course-supported system the facts above and this exact request:

> Propose two materially different operational interpretations of the Meridian Foundation
> mandate. For each interpretation, list the objective, hard constraints, assumptions you had to
> invent, validation tests, and the one CIO question that must be answered before optimization.
> Do not choose securities or produce an allocation.

Preserve both outputs separately. For every material claim, mark `supported`, `unsupported`, or
`requires clarification` and cite the exact governing fact. Agreement between systems does not
resolve the CIO's missing preference. If an interface is unavailable, use the published
`COURSE-RUN PROBE:` route with the exact common prompt and your prediction.
