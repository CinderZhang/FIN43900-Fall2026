## The deep read — MD&A first

*Fifteen minutes. Silent. This is the longest quiet block of the course so far.*

**MD&A = Management's Discussion and Analysis** — management explaining its own numbers in
plain English, in its own words. It is the fastest way to actually know a company.

**Where it is:**

- **10-K → Item 7** — "Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and
  Results of Operations"
- **10-Q → Item 2** — the same thing, shorter
- **Lost your file?** [SEC EDGAR full-text search](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/) →
  type your ticker → form type `10-K` → open the newest one.

Read for three things: **what the business actually sells · what management says changed
and why · what they warn you about.**

Take notes in your own words — in your `.md` or on paper. **Every note gets a locator.**

> The shape, not your answer *(training case)*: "MD&A, FY2024 10-K, Item 7: management
> explains the margin drop as freight and input costs, and says both eased in Q4.
> **My note:** the margin story is *cost*, not price — they never claim they lost pricing
> power. **What I want to check:** did freight actually ease, or is that guidance dressed
> up as fact?"

*Read it yourself. In fifteen minutes you will know something about this company that no
summary was ever going to give you.*

## Tell your partner the company

*3 minutes each way, then swap. From your reading — screens down.*

1. **What it is** — how it actually makes money
2. **What is good, and what is bad**
3. **Challenges, risks, advantages**
4. **Recent performance against its own history**
5. **The outlook**, if management gives one

**Listener, your job:** ask **one question the story didn't answer.** That question is
worth putting in the report.

> The shape, not your answer *(training case)*: "It's a mid-size industrial distributor —
> it buys parts from manufacturers and sells them to contractors, so it earns a spread on
> service and delivery speed, not on making anything. **Good:** it's taken share three
> years running and owns its branches. **Bad:** half of revenue is construction, so it
> moves with housing starts. **Risk:** one supplier is a fifth of purchases. **Against its
> own history:** margins are a point better than 2022, but the growth rate has halved.
> **Outlook:** management guides to low-single-digit growth and says destocking is over —
> which is exactly the claim I'd test first."

4. **Send it. Then go to the next slide's work while it drafts.**

> The shape, not your answer: "Here is the report I wrote in our last session [paste or
> attach your `.md`]. Rewrite it as a company research report for a reader who has not
> read the filing. **Keep my judgments — do not replace them with yours.** Mark every
> claim with its source (filing item and page · outlet and date · call date); where I gave
> you no source, write `UNSOURCED` instead of filling the gap yourself. End with my call —
> **watch-defer** — and one sentence naming what would change it. No price target."

*A gap you can see is worth more than a gap it quietly filled for you.*

## The rewrite — 2. Work while it drafts

*(~9 minutes. Two or three sources beyond your one filing.)*

1. **Prior 10-Ks — the same company page.**
   [SEC EDGAR full-text search](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/) → your ticker → form
   type `10-K` → open the **two years before** the one you already have. What did
   management promise then, and did it happen?
2. **Recent news — from the company itself.**
   Web-search `[company name] investor relations` → **News** or **Press releases**.
   Dated, attributable, easy to cite.
3. **A CEO interview or the earnings call.**
   Same investor-relations page → **Events** or **Quarterly results** → the call replay or
   transcript. Or search YouTube for `[company name] CEO interview`.

**The standard for a source:** dated · attributable · **and you can point at the sentence
you used.** A source you cannot cite where you used it is not a source.

> Keep a running `sources.md` as you go — link, date, and the one line you took from it.
> You will need that list twice today: in the report, and in the checkout.

## The rewrite — 3. Merge and verify

*(~7 minutes. Your draft finished while you were reading. Now you are the editor.)*

1. **Every claim keeps its source.** Anything marked `UNSOURCED` gets a source or gets cut.
2. **Your added sources go where you used them** — not in a pile at the end.
3. **The judgments are yours.** Any sentence stating an opinion you do not hold gets
   rewritten in your words or deleted.
4. **Your call is at the end**, with the one sentence that would change it.

> The shape, not your answer: "It wrote *'the company is well positioned in a growing
> market'* — I don't know that and neither does it. Cut. It wrote *'services revenue grew
> 13%'* with no source — I added `(10-K, Item 7, p. 31)`, because that's where I read it.
> It wrote *'we recommend accumulating'* — my call is watch-defer, so I rewrote the ending
> in my own words."

**The rewrite is the machine's. The report is yours.** In twenty minutes you paste a
footer that says exactly that — and it has to be true.
