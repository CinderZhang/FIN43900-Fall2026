# Lab 12 — Pro-Forma Presentation: the Message, the Page, and the Sceptic

**One thing today: put your valuation in front of a colleague and defend it with numbers and reasons.**

**Category:** Thursday merit checkout; anchors below.


**You arrive with:** your company's shock table, named case and price-implied values from Lab 11 ·
your draft two-line message · your Lab 11 chat · your partner, who is today's sceptic.

## Reopen and rerun

1. **VS Code → File → Open Recent** → your Course/Work Folder.
2. **Terminal → New Terminal.**
3. Run your what-if file. *Expect:* Tuesday's values.

## D — the question, the same for everyone

> **What do you tell a colleague about your company in a two-line message, on one page, and under
questioning — and what would change your mind?**

Paste it into your chat.

**Explain to your partner first:** read your two-line message aloud. Your partner says which of the
five parts is missing or weakest: value and price, what drives the gap, what breaks it, where you
stand, what you are watching. Fix it before anything else. Swap.

## R — the shape, from Part 2

| Surface | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Two-line message | value and price · what drives the gap · what breaks it · posture · what you watch |
| One page | assumption grid with labels · five years with the check block under them · rate table · shock table · price-implied table · three review triggers · the disclaimer |
| Eight questions | why that rate · terminal share · growth versus reported · your company's special line · what the market is saying · what would make you drop the case · why FCFE (or FCFF) · why not the data provider's numbers |
| Posture | a conditional evidence test, never buy or sell: "wait for proof of …", "revise if …" |

## I — the page and the answers, from one request

Send this to your chat, word for word, with your files attached to the conversation:

> From my pro-forma, my assumption table and my what-if results, write a one-page markdown summary
> in this order: two-line message; the assumption grid with labels and reasons; the five-year
> statements with the check block under them; the shock table; the named case; the price-implied
> table; three review triggers, each a number in a filing that would make me withdraw the case;
> the line "learning exercise, not investment research or financial advice". Then answer these eight
> questions in one or two sentences each, every answer containing a number from my files.

Paste the eight questions from the table above. Save the page in your open folder. Read every
number against your own output; fix any the AI invented. *Expect:* one page you could hand to a
portfolio manager, and eight answers with numbers.

## V — the sceptic

Your partner reads your page and asks the two hardest of the eight questions, plus one of their own.
You answer aloud, with the number, then write the three answers into your page. Swap roles. The
sceptic is graded on the difficulty of the questions; the presenter on the numbers in the answers.

## E — the panel

Send one more request: "Put my assumptions behind sliders in a small local app or notebook so a

the check block update." Run it once; move one slider; note the value in your page. Project 1
requires this surface; today you have seen it work.

## Floor

answered, and the panel run once. You submit individually.

## Presenting a valuation — Learn on your own

1. What is posture-first? 2. Why does a page carry the check block and the triggers? 3. Explain to
your partner the two challenges every presenter gets — "the terminal value is the whole answer" and
"the market disagrees with you" — and the answer to each.

## Reflect

change first if the next filing disappointed.

## Merit anchors — five criteria, 5 points each, 25 in total

**4** = one minor weakness; **2** = an important link incomplete; **1** = minimal; **0** = missing
or fabricated. Score one underlying defect once.

| Criterion | 5 | 3 | 0–1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Message and posture | five parts present; posture is a conditional evidence test | one part missing or posture vague | a buy or sell, or no posture |
| The page | every surface present with the check block and three triggers tied to filings | present but a trigger or the check block missing | numbers without sources or checks |
| Answers under questioning | eight answers, each with a number from the student's own files | answers present, some without numbers | generic or invented |
| Sceptic | three specific questions asked and the presenter's numbers checked | questions generic | missing |
| Panel and record | the panel runs, one move recorded; the locked record from Lab 11 carried onto the page | one of the two missing | neither |
