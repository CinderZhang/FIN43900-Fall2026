# Capstone — Integrated Financial Analysis System

You are the lead analyst. Build one system that joins your corporate-finance work and your
investment work into one committee decision. Recommend two things: the action, with its
conditions; and whether the committee should **approve, revise, defer, or reject the system for
decision use**.

Points and the enforced due date appear in the Brightspace assignment header. Edition A and Edition B are yours to keep and compare; neither is submitted.
Use AI. How you build it is yours.

## Base requirements

Every capstone meets all seven.

1. **The system runs.** A Python web app a stranger starts from your README and operates without
   you.
2. **Integration.** At least one corporate component (valuation, operating drivers, credit or
   downside) and at least one investment component (mandate, allocation, signal, scenario risk or
   monitoring) pass at least one material input between them. Reuse and repair your Project 1,
   Project 2 or Weeks 13–14 robo-advisor work; disclose what you reused. Do not build a new third
   system from scratch.
3. **At least one load-bearing generative-AI component** inside the system: one AI call that does
   work your code cannot, with a guard on its output and one documented case where you challenged
   or corrected it. The route is Purdue GenAI Studio's free API
   ([RCAC guide](https://www.rcac.purdue.edu/knowledge/genaistudio/api)); RCAC supports access,
   not the teaching team. If the API fails, the documented manual protocol is admissible: the
   exact prompt, the pasted output and your validation of it. The access route never changes the
   scoring standard.
4. **One Locked Changed-Input Record**, end to end: your AI-off prediction, committed to GitHub
   before the run, then the result.
5. **One cross-component test:** a contradiction between the components, or a failure of an input
   they share.
6. **The two-part recommendation**, what the system cannot establish, and the triggers that would
   reverse it.
7. **Two statements** open your README, your memo, each video and the launch-post draft: which AI
   you used and for what, and that the analysis and the decision are yours; education, not
   investment advice.

## Submit

1. The GitHub repository, with the AI component named by file and function in the manifest.
2. `docs/decision-memo.md` and `docs/validation-and-ai-use.md`, with the Locked Changed-Input
   Record.
3. Three videos as YouTube links, not video files, Unlisted or Public (your choice, never Private), face on camera,
   transcripts enabled: Video 1, how you built it with AI (6:00 at most); Video 2, the technical
   overview (10:00 at most); Video 3, the committee presentation, opening with your system running
   live (8:00 at most). Attach the transcripts, or link them in your repo.
4. `docs/launch-post.md`, a draft of about 800 words. Publishing it is optional, encouraged, and
   never graded.
5. The manifest: every location a GitHub link, except the videos.

Attach the same `.md` files to the Brightspace submission. Formats and the manifest's rows: the
[student checklist](major-project-student-checklist.md).

## Grading

There is no live board and no scheduled defense. The Weeks 15–16 meetings are consultation
studios, with no points. The 400-point capstone is graded holistically under the major-project
analytic rubric: a complete bundle earns evaluation, not points; a missing item is missing
evidence under the criterion it starves.

Ranked tiers will be announced before the capstone opens.
