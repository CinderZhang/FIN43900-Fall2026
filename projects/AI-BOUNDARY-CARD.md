# AI Boundary Card — Use This All Semester

This card controls the Edition A project checkpoints. A weekly lab may add a narrower AI-off phase,
but it cannot silently change these project rules.

## The short rule

1. Build the named Edition A evidence yourself during the published checkpoint window.
2. Submit it and verify the Brightspace receipt before project-specific generative-AI work.
3. Preserve Edition A unchanged.
4. After the receipt, use AI as permitted, disclose material use, verify consequential claims and
   code independently, and record why you accepted, modified, or rejected material suggestions.
5. Edition B may be much better, slightly different, or unchanged. You are graded on the quality
   of each edition and the evidence for the changes—not on producing a dramatic difference.

## Before the Edition A receipt

### Permitted

- course handouts, instructor demonstrations, textbooks, lecture notes, and primary filings;
- ordinary web or library search that returns sources rather than generated summaries;
- calculators and spreadsheet formulas you construct;
- non-generative syntax highlighting, file navigation, and version control;
- the supplied starter, known-answer fixtures, and documentation; and
- asking the instructor or TAs to clarify the rule or resolve an accessibility/technology issue.

### Not permitted for the student's own project

- chatbots, generative search summaries, AI research agents, or document summarizers;
- coding assistants, generative autocomplete, AI debugging, or generated spreadsheet formulas;
- AI-generated prose, model architecture, assumptions, securities, strategy, code, slides, or
  presentation scripts; and
- paraphrasing or translation tools that generate substantive project reasoning.

If a browser, editor, search engine, or operating system inserts a generative feature, turn it off
for the checkpoint or use the published non-generative route. When unsure, ask before relying on it.

## What “project-specific” means

The boundary applies when the tool receives or generates material for the student's own target,
portfolio, data, assumptions, code, decision, or deliverable.

- Week 1's assigned AI-assisted company-screening practice occurs before the Project 1 valuation
  boundary and does not invalidate Project 1 Edition A.
- Week 8 Lab 15's assigned two-AI comparison uses a common CIO case and does not invalidate
  Project 2 Edition A. Giving either model the student's own Project 2 proposal does.
- General course demonstrations do not invalidate an Edition A unless a student turns them into
  project-specific generated work before the receipt.

## Exact end event

The AI-off phase ends only after Brightspace shows the first successful receipt for the designated
Edition A assignment. Record its attempt/receipt identifier and timestamp. Where the checkpoint
requires a repository snapshot, record the matching commit. The published Project 1, Project 2,
and Capstone assignment pages control their respective Edition A availability windows; Special
Access controls an approved individual variation.

The receipt proves when the submitted version was frozen; it does not prove what tools were used
before submission. Project 1's supervised Lab 03 can supply observed pre-AI evidence. Project 2 and
Capstone Edition A are declared baselines, followed by supervised changed-input/evidence-navigation
work that tests individual command without relying on the declaration alone.

Each checkpoint page must publish how Special Access implements approved extended time or an
alternate setting and how a documented absence receives an equivalent bounded window. The
alternative must preserve the same Edition A freeze and receipt evidence without requiring public
disclosure of a diagnosis or private circumstances.

Do not overwrite Edition A. Corrections after the receipt go into a dated addendum or Edition B.

## If AI was used accidentally or too early

Do not fabricate an earlier version or conceal the event.

1. Stop project-specific AI use.
2. Preserve the actual work and, without copying private account data or a full chat, record the
   tool, approximate time, and affected part.
3. Contact the instructor/TA through the published support route before the checkpoint closes when
   feasible.
4. Follow the assigned recovery path. The original work remains the record; a reconstructed
   “clean” Edition A is not accepted as history.

Disclosure supports a fair boundary; it is not an automatic misconduct finding. Academic-
integrity determinations follow Purdue procedure, not an AI detector or writing style.

## After the receipt

AI may support the permitted research, implementation, validation planning, writing, and rehearsal
for that project. It does not become evidence merely by agreeing with another model or passing code
it helped write. For every load-bearing AI contribution, retain:

- the task and material output or concise trace;
- `accept`, `modify`, or `reject` plus the student's reason;
- the independent source, calculation, invariant, changed-input test, or counterexample used; and
- the resulting decision or revision.

The student remains responsible for locating evidence, explaining material logic, reproducing a
bounded result, and revising or withdrawing a claim when the evidence fails.
